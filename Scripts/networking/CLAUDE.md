Non-obvious constraints that cause subtle bugs if violated. Rates and wire format are in the Reference section above.

**Two clocks, and the input path uses the tick one.** `estimated_host_time()` is wall-derived (`Time.get_ticks_msec()`, 1 ms resolution). Godot runs physics from an accumulator *inside* the main loop, so at 60 fps with a 120 Hz tick TWO `_physics_process` steps execute back to back and only then does the frame render — and both read the same wall value. Anything produced once per tick and compared with strict inequality therefore breaks: input stamps collide, `RemoteController`'s dedupe (`state.host_timestamp <= newest_queued`) silently drops one, and a client on a display slower than the physics rate delivers fewer inputs than the host consumes. The queue starves, the drain fires, and the lead servo — whose error term is pop-overdue measured on that same wall clock — reads the frame-bunching offset as genuine lateness and integrates to `MAX_LEAD_EXTRA_S`. All four were observed together in one playtest: lead pinned at 50 ms on a 30 ms link, `input_starvation` triggering every auto-marker, drains to 52/s against a queue averaging 11 deep. **So `NetworkManager.sim_time()` — which advances exactly `TICK_DURATION` per step and is slewed (0.1 ms/tick) toward `estimated_host_time()` — owns everything produced or consumed per tick: input stamping, the host's consumption gate, the drain trigger, and pop-overdue.** Consecutive stamps are a tick apart by construction at any framerate; the slew is two orders of magnitude under a tick, so `sim_time()` is strictly increasing and collisions are impossible (pinned in `test_sim_clock.gd`). This is what Source gets from a tick number in the usercmd and Overwatch from numbering command frames — neither wall-stamps a per-tick event. **Render instants deliberately stay on `estimated_host_time()`**: a frame draws an arbitrary wall moment between ticks, and quantizing to the tick grid would stutter at high refresh rates (see the render-clock rule below). The two stay within the slew bound, so a render-time claim stamp read against the tick-time input timeline is still valid. When adding anything per-tick to the wire, ask which clock it belongs to — the answer is almost always the tick one.

**One render clock: `estimated_host_time() + input_lead` (v59).** Every rendered entity sits on the instant the local player's own predicted body already occupies — that body can't leave it without introducing local input delay, and claim stamps are already anchored there, so it is the only clock the scene can share. Remote skaters carry the lead in their forward-predict depth (`LagCompRewind.forward_predict_ticks(fraction, interp_delay, lead)`, landing on `(H − d) + fraction × (d + L)`); the loose puck predicts to the same target; `self_view_time` and `puck_view_time` now return the *same* instant by design. The host reproduces each depth from the claim-carried `input_lead_ms`, bounded once in `LagCompRewind.clamped_lead_s` so a modified client can't buy a different rewind in one consumer than another — **never re-derive the lead locally at a call site.** The host never has to *look up* that instant: remote bodies are reconstructed by computation from a `remote_view_time` snapshot, and the loose-puck lookup is covered because `DeferredClaimQueue` holds the claim until the buffer reaches it. **What licenses this at all is skating's acceleration ceiling**, and that makes it a gameplay constraint rather than a lucky fact. A remote body can only diverge from its prediction by changing thrust, so the error grows as `½·a·t²` — with `a_max ≈ 10.5 m/s²`, ~9 cm over a 92 ms window. A game with instantaneous lateral movement diverges as `v·t`, as a step rather than a ramp (~46 cm over the same window at 5 m/s), which is why every FPS interpolates remote bodies and none forward-predict them. **Any future mechanic that lets a skater change velocity discontinuously — a dash, a teleport, a hard-cut that bypasses momentum — breaks this clock**, and would read as remote bodies snapping during exactly that move. The one discontinuity that already exists is handled the right way and is the template: a body check's `stagger_timer` is replicated in `SkaterNetworkState` and fed to `integrate_forward` on BOTH the render and the rewind, so the thrust penalty is reproduced rather than predicted through. The goalie is deliberately NOT yet on this clock — it still renders at `H − d` (see the goalie invariant below), which is the largest remaining coherence gap and is tracked as its own piece of work. Mixed builds must be refused: nothing on the wire changed, so a version mismatch mis-adjudicates silently rather than failing to decode.

**A client's puck is always in one of these modes** (checked in this priority order in `PuckController._physics_process`): *Local carry* — the local player carries; puck pinned to their blade. *Provisional carry* — an uncontested optimistic pin to the local blade awaiting host confirmation (visual only; see "No pickup prediction…" decision); rolls back on timeout. *Remote carry* — a remote player carries; puck pinned to **that skater's interpolated blade** (`get_carry_target_global`) rather than interpolating from its own buffer, so the puck shares the carrier's render timeline and stays glued to the stick. *Predicted* (the one loose-puck mode, Phase 3/4b) — the client runs the host's own analytic sim (`PuckAuthorityRules.step_frame_substep`, the literally-shared step) to `estimated_host_time()` + its input lead every frame (the shared render clock above): stateless re-predict from the newest snapshot — or, for the shooter's own release until the host's snapshots reflect it, from the **local release seed** (see the release-seed decision above) — with goalie contact held as a prediction stop, no goal ever predicted (inbound pucks park on the goal line), and residuals absorbed by the shared SmoothDamp tail. The puck is a plain `Node3D` — no physics body on any peer — and contacts are detected analytically inside the prediction loop. Loose-puck claim rewinds correspondingly read the claim stamp via `LagCompRewind.puck_view_time`, so render == rewind holds at present. *Interpolated* — the stale-data fallback only (newest snapshot older than `Constants.PUCK_PREDICT_MAX_S`, e.g. deep loss or clock warmup); client renders from `estimated_host_time() - interpolation_delay`, a full `interp_delay` behind host-present. Both loose modes run the same velocity-feed-forward `_smooth_damp` position smoother (advanced by the puck's own velocity for zero steady-state lag — critical here, a fast loose puck would otherwise trail by metres on a shot/pass — with only the residual error damped, and the velocity-aware `needs_hard_snap` guard deciding teleports). The smoother is **reseeded from the puck's live position on every fresh entry into the loose modes** (carry / pin → loose), which is what makes those hand-offs seamless and **subsumes the former rejoin-blend**. **Smart (board-aware) extrapolation** (fallback path only): the forward dead-reckon is gated by `_crosses_board` (`stop_extrapolation_at_boards`) — if the projected position would land past the inner board boundary (`GameRules.clamp_to_rink_inner`, rounded corners exact), the lead is dropped for that frame and the puck holds at the newest authoritative position rather than projecting through the boards and snapping back on the host's reflected samples. All pin modes keep buffering host snapshots (`apply_state` early-returns only for the local-carry pin) so the buffer stays warm across the pin — and a live release seed depends on that intake for its handover. The `carrier_idx` field decoded from world state is rendered-state only; the reliable `notify_carrier_changed` RPC is what installs/clears the remote-carry pin (via `GameManager._on_remote_carrier_changed` → `notify_remote_pickup`).

**On clients, the carrier view (`PuckController._client_carrier_peer_id` + the carrier-skater pins) is managed exclusively by carrier *events*, never by world state.** Unreliable packet ordering conflicts with locally-predicted carrier transitions. On the server, `_carrier_peer_id` in `PuckController` is set by physics callbacks (`_on_puck_picked_up` / `_on_puck_released`) and is host-only — `puck.carrier` / `_carrier_peer_id` are **never** set on clients. The client-side view is written only by the event-driven notify paths (`notify_local_pickup` / `notify_remote_pickup` / `notify_remote_carrier_changed` / the release, nudge, and drop notifies) and read via `get_client_carrier_peer_id()` / `get_client_carrier_skater()`; `LocalController`'s claim gate branches on it (loose → pickup claim, opposing carrier → poke/stick-lift claim). **Presentation code — camera, HUD, anything that asks "who has it" outside host-only logic — reads `GameManager.get_puck_carrier()`, which tries the client view first and falls back to `puck.carrier` for the host's view of remote carriers.** Reaching for `puck.get_carrier()` directly from a peer-agnostic call site is a silent bug, not a crash: it reports "loose" forever on every machine except the host, which is how carrier camera framing and the offside ghost banner sat dead for clients. The world state does encode a `carrier_idx` field, but clients use it only inside the loose-puck prediction (the release-seed handover keys on the newest snapshot going loose) — they do not write the carrier view from it. (Carrier events arrive via a reliable RPC *or* the snapshot-piggybacked event block — see the `SnapshotEventLog` invariant below — but both funnel into the same `NetworkManager` signals, so the "events, not world state" rule is unchanged.)

**Carrier events ride two channels, deduped and ordered by `event_seq` (`SnapshotEventLog`).** A lost reliable packet costs a retransmit round-trip — 100+ ms of episodic latency on exactly the carrier-change moments that matter most — so the host also appends its last ~1 s of carrier events (pickup grant / carrier change / steal / drop) as a trailing block on every unreliable world-state packet (u8 count **last**, 13 B records before it, appended in `_broadcast_state` *after* the replay recorder takes its copy — PackedByteArray COW keeps replay frames event-free). Clients apply the block in `receive_world_state` *before* the world-state decode (event and matching state land in the same frame), filtered by target peer and gated by a monotonic seq watermark; the four reliable backstop RPCs carry the same seq and are dropped when the watermark has passed them, so a late retransmit can never regress carrier state that a newer snapshot event already advanced. The watermark advances over *every* record in a block, including events targeted at other peers — the seq space is global, so a skipped not-for-me seq is not a gap. The log is **connection-scoped**: `reset()` replaces it, `prepare_for_new_game()` deliberately does not (a rematch keeps client watermarks alive; restarting seq would silently drop every event of the next match). When adding a new latency-critical event, add an `EventType`, record it in the `send_*` wrapper, and gate its reliable handler with `try_apply_reliable` — do not invent a second dedupe scheme.

**Lag-comp rewind goes through `LagCompRewind` — never reach into raw timestamps.** Two helpers encode the two view perspectives every claim handler needs: `self_view_time(host_timestamp)` for entities the claimant rendered via local prediction (their own skater body/blade), and `remote_view_time(host_timestamp, interp_delay_ms)` for entities they rendered via interpolation (other skaters, loose puck, remote-carried puck, goalie). Each claim resolver picks per entity — pickup/poke rewind the blade as self and puck as remote; hit rewinds the hitter as self and victim+puck as remote; shot-release rewinds only the **goalie** as remote — the firing **origin** is not rewound at all any more (see *Shot lag compensation* below: the host replays the release input, so its own blade on that tick already is the release pose). The one-timer adds a third, simpler perspective: the loose puck its shooter aimed at sits exactly one `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` behind the tick the host replays the swing on — a fixed, ping-independent offset, read straight from the world-state buffer by `RemoteController.sample_shooter_puck_view`. Rewind depth is RTT-independent (depends only on `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` and the client-reported `interp_delay_ms`), so two players reaching for the same puck are arbitrated by their stamped view-times instead of by network luck. Open-coded `host_timestamp ± rtt/2` formulas are a class of bug — the wrong shift, and they couple validation to ping.

**`rtt_ms` on the host release path is the host's OWN ping measurement for the shooter** (`NetworkManager.get_peer_ping_ms`), not a client-claimed number — no shot RPC exists any more, so nothing about a release is self-reported. It does **not** advance the puck forward (that release-time RTT/2 advance was removed — see *Shot lag compensation* below); it reconstructs the shooter's interpolation delay for the goalie rewind, and gates "is this a remote shooter at all" (`rtt_ms`/`interp_delay_ms` of 0 is the host's own player or a bot). `ShotReleaseRules.clamp_rtt_ms` stays on the path as defense-in-depth. On the shooter's client RTT is not consumed by the release at all (Phase 4b: the release seed predicts on the shared host-time clock, so no RTT projection exists), and no rewind reads it — claim chains dropped `rtt_ms` once the LagCompRewind helper landed.

**Shot lag compensation has two independent pieces, with no release-time forward advance.** *Host-blade origin:* the host fires every authoritative release from its own blade on the tick it replays the shooter's release input (`Skater.get_blade_contact_global`) — that tick IS the shooter's release pose, because the host simulates the same inputs. **No caller may pass a fire point, and none does.** `puck.release()` seats the puck itself, and it is the only code that knows a slapper's puck sits on its ice pin rather than at the blade lifted over the shoulder — an origin computed by the caller and applied after `release()` silently un-does that, which is what put remote slapshots on the wind-up blade's x/z. *Goalie reaction back-date + goalie-position rewind (remote-view):* the goalie's reaction timers are back-dated to the instant the shooter perceived the release, and the goalie is rewound to the interpolated position they rendered it at, for fair release geometry. That instant is one `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` before the tick the host replays the release on — the client applies an input immediately and stamps it a lead ahead, the host holds it until the stamp comes round — so **both** remote release paths subtract the lead before handing the timestamp to `clamp_back_date` / `remote_view_time` (`_on_remote_derived_release`, `_on_remote_derived_one_timer`). Anchoring at the host's own tick instead makes the back-date come out ~0 and stops the position rewind a lead short of what the shooter rendered. *No RTT/2 advance:* the host (and the shooter's own client) does **not** shove the puck forward by `velocity * rtt/2` at release — both fire from the same blade on the same solver and the client predicts to `estimated_host_time()`, so prediction and authority line up without it; the old advance only bought close-shot rebound alignment at the cost of a visible 1-tick puck pop and a non-swept teleport that could skip the puck past a close goalie/boards. Close-shot goalie contacts are simply the prediction stop: the predicted puck holds at the pad and the authoritative save outcome folds in on the next snapshots.

**A cancel deadline the host arms for a remote must outlive the one that remote arms for itself.** The caught one-timer's window (`one_timer_window_duration`) is the live case: the host arms it on the tick it grants the pickup, the carrier's own client arms it a one-way trip later when the grant RPC lands, and that client's release then takes an input lead to reach the host — so in input-stamp space the client's window sits `one_way + INPUT_LEAD_SEC` later than the host's. The host therefore holds its deadline open by exactly that offset (`SkaterController.one_timer_window_lag_grace`, overridden only on `RemoteController`, formula in `ShotReleaseRules.one_timer_window_grace`), and both sides tick the window one input per tick, so they cancel on the SAME input stamp. Every peer arms the honest `one_timer_window_duration` for the carrier it predicts — inflating the CLIENT's copy instead (the old `+ rtt/2`) is the wrong side of the seam: it lengthens a window whose end the host does not honour, and a release in the overhang lands on a skater the host has already cancelled back to carry, so the shot silently never happens while the client predicted it. The slack is one-sided on purpose — too little grace eats shots, too much only leaves the host briefly holding a wind-up the client dropped.

**Puck contacts are detected analytically inside the prediction loop** — the puck is a plain `Node3D` with no physics body, so there are no engine contact signals anywhere. Goalie contact is a prediction STOP (hold at the contact; the host's save logic makes any local rebound wrong in kind); post/net/board contacts are the shared solver's own geometry, so the predicted bounce IS the host's. Full rationale in the release-seed decision above.

**The goalie AI is host-only; clients render it purely from the interpolated host pose broadcast** (full pose on the wire — see Reference → Goalie Networking). Do not reintroduce client-side goalie AI or `apply_state_transition`/`apply_shot_reaction`-style reliable RPCs: the earlier client-AI-with-soft-correction model caused ghost saves and phantom goals when the client's local puck read disagreed with the host. The host's `GoalieShotReaction.started` signal stays host-internal (arms the slide event-lockout).

**Reconcile saves and restores only narrow shot-state fields** (`_state`, follow-through timers, one-timer window, the one-timer **retention** timer, `slapper_charge_timer`, and the wrister swing state — `swing_rotation`, the `cursor_speed_ema` power signal, and the `stroke_travel` power-ceiling gate). The `slapper_charge_timer` save/restore is required because `_update_slapper_charge` ticks the timer inside the replay loop — without it each reconcile re-ticks the unconfirmed inputs and the timer inflates O(N) per broadcast, popping the blade above `slapper_wind_up_height`. The wrister swing save/restore is the same shape (replay re-runs the unconfirmed sweep, evolving the chirality/travel accumulators and perturbing the cursor-speed EMA). Server authority on shot **state**: if `server_state.shot_state` differs after replay, server wins — `_state` is overwritten. The wrister swing state is **not** server-overwritten: it is a local control input (the player's precision sweep), so the host's lagged value is never imported — doing so rubber-banded the live release power. **Three guards protect in-flight shot transitions:**
- **`FOLLOW_THROUGH` → aim state blocked.** When the client is in `FOLLOW_THROUGH` it has already sent the release RPC; the host is processing-lag behind. Reverting would loop the follow-through animation every broadcast.
- **`WRISTER_AIM` / `SLAPPER_CHARGE_WITH_PUCK` → skating blocked when `has_puck = true`.** There is a ~15ms window between the client pressing shoot and the host processing that input (120Hz batch window + network transit). World states broadcast at 60Hz are very likely to fire during this window carrying `shot_state = SKATING_WITH_PUCK`. Without this guard reconcile resets the state machine on every broadcast; `shoot_pressed` has already fired once and `shoot_held` never re-enters aim, so the shot never releases. Gated on `has_puck` so a puck steal (which clears `has_puck` before the next reconcile) still overrides correctly.
- **`ONE_TIMER_RETENTION` is client-owned outright — server shot state never applies while the client is in it.** The only blanket guard here rather than an enumeration, and safe to be one *because the hold self-terminates* within `one_timer_retention_time`: a wrong local state survives a fraction of a second before its own timer expires into `FOLLOW_THROUGH`. (`FOLLOW_THROUGH` has no such bound, which is why it enumerates instead.) Both directions are wrong to apply: the lagged pre-release state re-arms a wind-up the player already released (the held button can't re-fire the rising-edge entry) or ejects the swing to skating, while a server `FOLLOW_THROUGH` silently EATS the shot on the client's own screen — it skips its own release, so its puck prediction never sees the redirect (the host still fires it off the input stream, so the shot itself survives; the shooter just watches it appear late). That second case should be unreachable (the client applies inputs immediately while the host gates consumption on `host_timestamp`, so the client leads by the input lead and its hold always expires first), but it fails invisibly, so it isn't worth betting the shot on. Stoppages still cancel the swing — that runs through `teleport_to` → `_cancel_active_charge`, not through reconcile.
- **`SLAPPER_CHARGE_WITHOUT_PUCK` → `SKATING_WITHOUT_PUCK` blocked.** The puckless one-timer wind-up has no puck, so the `has_puck` guard above never covers it: the server's lagged `SKATING_WITHOUT_PUCK` (slap press not yet processed there) would eject the wind-up and the held RMB can't re-fire the rising-edge entry. Real progressions still apply — the puck arriving flips the server to `SLAPPER_CHARGE_WITH_PUCK`, a release to `FOLLOW_THROUGH`, neither of which is blocked.

**The wrister swing/charge accumulators are saved before replay and restored after — the replay's pass through the unconfirmed inputs is discarded.** The cursor-speed power signal and swing chirality are functions of the per-tick cursor delta, so replay re-evolving them would perturb the live signal; the save/restore (which superseded the former first/last-input mouse-position seeding) keeps the live tick state authoritative instead.

**Board collision is clamped in the reconcile replay loop** via `GameRules.clamp_to_rink_inner` after each `global_position += velocity * delta` step. Without this, board-bounce divergence triggers a feedback loop of reconciles.

**Reconcile uses trajectory comparison, not position-snapshot comparison.** Every physics tick on `LocalController._physics_process`, after `_process_input` mutates skater state, a `PredictedState` snapshot is appended to `_prediction_history` keyed by the input's `host_timestamp` (capped at `PHYSICS_TICK × 2` entries ≈ 2 s). When `reconcile(server_state)` fires, `PredictedState.find_at(_prediction_history, server_state.last_processed_host_timestamp)` returns the snapshot for that exact instant. The threshold check (`ReconciliationRules.skater_needs_reconcile`) compares **predicted-vs-server at the same `host_timestamp`** instead of current-client-vs-server, which subtracts the natural prediction lead (RTT/2 + `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` × velocity ≈ 0.33–0.6 m at skating speeds) out of the divergence. Thresholds can therefore stay tight (`reconcile_position_threshold = 0.05 m`, `reconcile_velocity_threshold = 0.3 m/s`) — only true non-determinism (body-check mis-replay, contested collision resolution) trips them. After the lookup, `_prediction_history` is trimmed by the same predicate as `_input_history` (`host_timestamp > last_processed_host_timestamp`), since future reconciles only ask about strictly later timestamps. Cleared alongside `_input_history` on `teleport_to` and dead-puck-frozen-stick drains. **The replay both seeds from and re-records the history:** the replay loop runs under the ACK-TIME shot-machine baseline (`server_state.shot_state` — the state the host evolved these same inputs from; transitions re-fire from the replayed input edges with `is_replaying` suppressing side effects), and each replayed input's `PredictedState` is re-recorded from the corrected trajectory — the stale pre-correction snapshots would otherwise re-trip the threshold on every subsequent ack in the unacked span, turning one real divergence into ~an RTT window of redundant reconciles. When no match is found (history capped before a reconcile catches up, post-teleport gap, NTP warmup) the comparison falls back to the live position so reconcile remains a safety net through edge cases. **Stale-ack gate:** reconcile early-outs unless `last_processed_host_timestamp` has advanced since the last one (`ReconciliationRules.ack_is_new`, epsilon-tolerant for the 0.1 ms wire grid). The host broadcasts every tick but only advances the ack when it pops a *due* input, so consecutive snapshots routinely repeat the same ack; the first reconcile at ack T matches its prediction and then trims it, so re-running at the repeated ack would `find_at`-miss, fall back to the (prediction-lead-ahead) live position, and fire a spurious snap — the dominant source of false reconcile churn on a clean LAN. Reset alongside `_prediction_history` clears.

**Blade and top-hand positions on remote skaters are extrapolated from the body velocity field**, not from derived position deltas. Dividing position deltas by client receive-time gaps amplifies jitter into visible blade jumps.

**Remote entities render at the lag-comp rewind instant.** *(Stage-3 experimental amendment: on this branch "the rewind instant" is the FORWARD-PREDICTED instant — remote skaters render intent-integrated from the interpolated-past base toward host-present via `SkaterMovementRules.integrate_forward` at `Constants.REMOTE_FORWARD_PREDICT_FRACTION` (1.0), and every carrier-anchored claim resolver (hit, poke, stick-lift) reconstructs the identical prediction host-side through `LagCompRewind.forward_predict_skater` — same primitive, same shared constants, intent quantized through the wire codec. Render == rewind therefore still holds, at the predicted instant. The loose puck renders through Phase-3/4b prediction instead of any lead (the stage-4 eased-lead machinery was deleted in Phase 4a, and the separate shooter trajectory mode + handoff slew in Phase 4b — superseded outright). At fraction 0 everything below reads exactly as shipped.)* `RemoteController._interpolate` and `PuckController` interpolate at `render_time = estimated_host_time() − interpolation_delay`: pure interpolate-in-the-past, a full `interp_delay` behind host-present. This is deliberate and load-bearing — it makes the rendered position **equal** `LagCompRewind.remote_view_time(host_ts, interp_delay)`, the instant the host rewinds to when validating pickup / poke / stick-lift / hit claims (the claim sends report the ADAPTED `get_interpolation_delay()` — the value that actually positioned the render that frame — not the target, which can lead the adapted value by tens of ms mid-jitter). **Render == rewind**, so the puck (or victim) you reach for on screen is exactly the one the host tests. (One deliberate, transient exception: while a local release seed is live (~one-way after a shot) the loose puck renders from the client's own release instant, which can sit slightly ahead of the host's — the optimistic pickup pin is gated off for exactly that window in `try_provisional_pickup`.) A non-zero lead breaks that: it renders remotes closer to host-present while the resolvers still rewind a full `interp_delay`, so the host validated grabs against a remote position up to `interp_delay/2` behind where the client reached — ~9 cm at skating speed, blowing out to 30–100 cm when `interp_delay` spikes on jitter, which is why contested pickups failed worst during laggy scrambles. (History: a `0.5` skater+puck lead shipped to close the chase gap and the body-check collision-prediction gap below; it desynced render from the claim rewind *and* rode the buffer edge, extrapolating nearly every frame — the snap-back the `is_extrapolating` canary is meant to catch. Removed in favor of render == rewind; the loose puck was additionally a drift from this doc's own "puck renders on the shared past instant" rule.)

**Body-check contact geometry must be reconstructed identically by all three parties.** Remote bodies are what the *local* player's predicted physics step collides against (`Skater._resolve_player_collisions`, analytic), and body checks are predicted client-side, so the same contact is computed three times: the client's LIVE step (against the rendered remote — stage-3 forward-predicted to ~host-present in `RemoteController._interpolate`), the client's RECONCILE REPLAY (against `RemoteController.sample_state_at`), and the host's HIT-CLAIM REWIND (`LagCompRewind.forward_predict_skater`). All three now run the SAME `integrate_forward` primitive with the same decay constant and `has_puck = false` convention — do not let one of them drift. `sample_state_at` is queried at an INPUT STAMP (`now + input lead`), so it is always past the newest buffered snapshot; it projects forward to the requested instant rather than freezing there. It used to freeze, which was conservative when remotes rendered in the past, but became the odd one out once the render gained forward prediction: replay re-resolved contacts against a body `one_way + broadcast_interval/2` older than both the screen and the host, enough at skating speed (vs a 0.7 m contact diameter) to flip overlap, the aggressor gate, and the contact normal. The freeze survives only past `_SAMPLE_PREDICT_MAX_S` as the deep-loss fallback. **A residual mismatch remains and is tracked as a GitHub issue:** the client's live step resolves its local body (predicted to `stamp = now + input lead`) against remotes rendered at `now`, so live contact geometry is one input lead off the host's, which resolves both at the stamp. Closing that needs collision to read a separately-predicted target from the render pose — the render instant is pinned by render == rewind for claims and cannot simply move.

**`Skater.render_transform()` is the only correct read for anything drawn onto a body.** `global_transform` is the post-tick pose, up to a tick of travel from where the body is actually being drawn, so on-ice chrome — slot ring, chevrons, nameplate, stamina gauge, slapper reticle, skate marks — must read `render_transform()`, a per-drawn-frame memo of `get_global_transform_interpolated()`. It is memoized because the readers' `_process` order is not fixed and they must all land on one frame's pose. A node placed that way must also opt OUT of physics interpolation (`PHYSICS_INTERPOLATION_MODE_OFF`), or the engine interpolates an already-interpolated pose. **The converse is the trap:** a node that stays ATTACHED to the body must NOT opt out, because an opted-out child renders against the body's post-tick position while the body renders interpolated — which is why the skater's stick and arm rig stays interpolated and accepts one tick of latency on its render-rate gait pose rather than sampling it more often.

**Correction smoothing (retained, independent of the lead).** After the render target is computed, the collision body position is advanced by the target's *own* velocity each frame (**zero steady-state lag** — smoothing the absolute position instead trails a moving body by ~`velocity × smooth_time`, multi-metre on a fast puck) and only the *residual error* is critically damped toward the target (`_smooth_damp`, `position_smooth_time`). So when a contradicting snapshot lands or the interp↔extrap branch flips, the error blends out instead of snapping, while straight-line motion tracks exactly. The smoother **subsumes the old extrapolation rejoin-blend**; a large per-tick delta (teleport / faceoff / goal reset) snaps it rather than sliding the body across the rink. The packet-loss `is_extrapolating` branch (render_time past the newest sample, capped at `extrapolation_max_ms`) is unchanged. `velocity` on the remote body stays the authoritative interpolated value (the smoother's internal velocity is display-only) so the host-side closing-velocity read is unaffected. **Lever B (`start_knockback_lead`):** a credited body check adds a brief 0→peak→0 `sin(t·π)` position pulse along the hit direction (`_knockback_lead_*`, ≤~0.22 m over ~120 ms), applied as a final additive **after** the SmoothDamp so the punch stays sharp.

**The shared interpolation-delay target is `rtt/2 + broadcast_interval + PDV spread`** (`network_manager.gd`, `_compute_target_interpolation_delay`), where the spread is `get_packet_delay_spread_ms()` — the de-clumped Jacobson PDV (`mean + 4·dev` of each packet's delay measured against its own host-capture stamp). It replaced a conservative `jitter_p95 × 2.0`: the old arrival-gap p95 couldn't tell genuine path jitter from Steam relay **clumping** (several snapshots land together, then a gap), so it needed a 2.0× bump that over-cushioned a *clean* link with pure render latency on every remote entity. The PDV spread times each packet against its host-capture stamp, so clumping barely moves it; it measures the steady path component at 1.0× and leans on the aggressive +10 ms/packet up-clamp in `adapt_interpolation_delay` to absorb transient clumps. **Watch on a real high-latency link:** `Guessing ahead` (F3) is the canary — now reported as the **share of frames extrapolating** (`extrapolation_pct`) rather than a raw `/s` rate, which was framerate-dependent and uncomparable across machines (a 240 fps client counted 4× a 60 fps one for identical buffer health; `client_fps` rides alongside to surface that). If the fraction climbs the cushion is under-sizing (buffer underrunning between clumps faster than the up-clamp can chase, visible as rhythmic snap-back on remote skaters); the one-line revert is to restore `get_jitter_p95() * 2.0` in `_compute_target_interpolation_delay`. With no render-time lead (above) this should sit near zero in steady state — the render point no longer rides the buffer edge, so a rising fraction is a genuine underrun rather than the old by-design lead extrapolation.

**Post-reconcile blade pose dispatches by state on the local controller.** After replay completes, `LocalController.reconcile` re-applies the blade based on the restored `_sm.get_state()`: slapper-charge states call `_apply_slapper_blade_position`, follow-through calls the matching `_apply_*_follow_through`, shot-blocking is a no-op (block stance owns the pose), everything else calls `_apply_blade_from_mouse`. Calling `_apply_blade_from_mouse` unconditionally would IK the blade to the mouse position every reconcile, popping it down from the slapper wind-up pose at the broadcast rate (60Hz) and producing a visible flicker against the next physics tick's slapper handler.

**Body check impulse is injected into the reconcile replay at the matching `host_timestamp`**, so replay reproduces the post-collision trajectory without oscillation.

**`LocalController.reconcile` is blocked during dead-puck phases.** `on_faceoff_positions` RPC is the authoritative source of skater positions during locked phases.

**Client inputs are applied immediately (no local delay) and stamped with `NetworkManager.estimated_input_stamp_time()`.** This stamps each input for `estimated_host_time() + INPUT_LEAD_SEC + lead_extra` where `INPUT_LEAD_SEC = BATCH_INTERVAL + BUFFER_TICKS * TICK_DURATION ≈ 25ms` and `lead_extra` is ClockSync's **adaptive servo** (0..`MAX_LEAD_EXTRA_S` 50 ms, initial one tick): the client measures how overdue its inputs were when the host popped them — each snapshot's freshly-advanced ack reveals it as `host_ts − ack stamp`, zero extra wire — and steps the extra by `mean overdue − one tick of grace`, fast up / slow down (the first playtest measured the static 25 ms lead marginal: median-0 host queue, pops 25–40 ms overdue, ~1 fallback tick/s). **The servo target must stay above pop-overdue's own floor.** Overdue is one-sided — `max(0, arrival − stamp) + tick quantization` — so lead can zero the lateness term but never the quantization term, and the mean floors at ~`TICK/2`. An earlier form servoed Jacobson `mean + 4·dev` toward the same one-tick grace; `4·dev` alone floors at ~`TICK`, so that error term could not reach zero at *any* lead and the integrator wound to `MAX_LEAD_EXTRA_S` on every link, including a perfect one (measured: pinned at the 50 ms cap for the full duration of three sessions on a 23 ms / 0 %-loss link — a permanent 50 ms input-latency tax, a host input queue at 3× its designed depth, and the body-check residual below parked at its worst case). Restoring a variance margin by moving it out of the error does not work either: any margin the integrator can observe reduces measured overdue and is simply backed out of `lead_extra`, leaving the fixed point unchanged. Burst tolerance comes from the fast-up/slow-down asymmetry, which is not a bias and does not move the fixed point. Overdue samples > 250 ms are phase-resume artifacts (an input parked across a replay) and are excluded. **A drained ack is not a servo sample either.** `_drain_backlog` acks several inputs in one tick, so the ack jumps by at least `trigger − target` (~25 ms) in a single snapshot and the last drained stamp reads hugely overdue; feeding that to the servo double-responds, because the drain already cleared the backlog, so the extra lead buys nothing and is charged straight to input latency while the integrator winds to `MAX_LEAD_EXTRA_S` and stays there. `NetworkManager` detects it client-side with no wire change — a healthy ack advances by ~`1/STATE_RATE` per snapshot, so anything at drain scale (`_ACK_DRAIN_ADVANCE_S`, 3 ticks) is a drain or an equally-not-a-lead-problem burst of lost snapshots. The normal advance must stay under that bound, which holds while `STATE_RATE >= 40`. The base lead accounts for worst-case 120Hz batch-send delay (≈8.3ms) plus a 2-tick buffer; `estimated_host_time()` already encodes RTT/2 via the NTP offset — adding RTT/2 again would double-count transit. Because the lead now varies, **claims carry the lead the client stamped with** (`input_lead_ms` on the four blade/hit claim RPCs, v39) and `LagCompRewind.self_view_time` rewinds with the carried value, bounded to `[INPUT_LEAD_SEC, INPUT_LEAD_SEC + MAX_LEAD_EXTRA_S]` so a modified client can't shove its self-view rewind forward. The lead servo lives in ClockSync as separate state — the NTP offset stays pure (see the ClockSync invariant).

**The host gates input consumption on `host_timestamp`.** `RemoteController._drive_from_input` only pops the front input when `host_timestamp <= NetworkManager.estimated_host_time()`. Without this gate, inputs are consumed immediately on arrival regardless of their timestamp, the queue empties between batches, and fallback-input fires for several ticks per gap. The gate falls through (`input_due = true`) when the clock is not yet ready so behaviour is unchanged during NTP warmup. When the queue is empty and not movement-locked, `NetworkTelemetry.record_input_starvation()` is called; when an input is popped, `record_input_lead(estimated_host_time() - input.host_timestamp)` records how overdue it was (target: near-zero ms, ideally ≤8.3ms). **Backlogs are drained, not carried:** consumption is one input per tick and production is one per tick, so a jitter-burst backlog (queue empties for N ticks, delayed inputs then land together) could never catch up on its own — every later input applied ~N ticks stale for the rest of the session, ratcheting with each burst. `_drain_backlog` acks-without-applying inputs overdue past `_DRAIN_TRIGGER_S` (~8 ticks) down to `_DRAIN_TARGET_S` (~2 ticks), folding edge flags (presses) into the next applied input so a press inside the dropped span still fires; held/absolute state is deliberately not carried. The client sees one reconcile correction for the dropped span — the honest cost of late delivery. **The trigger is sized against the measured steady state, not the theoretical one:** `host_input_queue_depth` runs 9-10 (the stamp-lead cushion working) while the front pops ~12 ms overdue, because the host renders ~100 fps against a 120 Hz tick so physics ticks bunch two to a frame and a pop lands up to a frame after its due instant. The original 4-tick/1-tick pair sat inside that band — it fired 4-12/s on a *healthy deep queue*, and drained to below the quantization floor so the next frame re-armed it. The trade is asymmetric: tolerating slip costs input LATENCY (invisible, bounded, corrected when it grows), draining costs a VISIBLE correction — so the trigger clears the quantization + hitch band and only catches a genuine ratchet. `record_input_drain()` / `input_drains_per_sec` (F3 "Backlog drains") counts it; read it WITH `host_input_queue_depth` — draining a DEEP queue means the trigger is too tight, draining a SHALLOW one means inputs genuinely arrive late (lead/clock work).

**The pending input queue is drained on `is_movement_locked()` phases.** `is_input_blocked()` returns immediately without draining — there is no longer a queue to flush since inputs are applied the frame they are gathered.

**The loose-puck smoother's snap guard is velocity-aware, and it is the only discrete correction left** (`PuckHandoffRules.needs_hard_snap`, run every frame by `_smooth_apply_and_prune`). Only cross-track error at `_SMOOTH_SNAP_DIST`, or along-track error beyond velocity × `_ALONG_SNAP_TIME_S`, teleports the rendered puck; below `_HANDOFF_MIN_SPEED` it degrades to the plain distance check (covers faceoff/goal-reset teleports at rest). A moving-target snap is recorded as `puck_hard_snaps` telemetry (genuine prediction divergence — want ~0); at-rest snaps are legitimate repositions and aren't counted. The old three-zone trajectory reconcile and the trajectory→interpolation render-time slew (`timeline_lead`) were deleted in Phase 4b — every loose-puck render target sits at ~host present, so there is no cross-timeline handoff left to ease; seams (release-seed handover, prediction↔fallback) are along-track offsets the guard deliberately tolerates and the SmoothDamp absorbs.

**Body check impulse uses `body_check_impulse_applied(impulse)`, not `body_checked_player`.** `Skater` emits `body_check_impulse_applied` symmetrically: the attacker emits its rebound delta from its own `body_check_delta = velocity - vel_after_slide` capture (`skater.gd:645-647`), and the same `_resolve_player_collisions` loop also captures `other.velocity` before the transfer `-=` and emits the victim's transfer delta directly on `other` (`skater.gd:720-727`). Both sides' `LocalController` connect to the signal and inject the captured impulse into reconcile replay at the matching `host_timestamp`. Without the victim emission, only attacker rebound was captured and the victim relied entirely on snapshot authority absorbing the impulse — broken if the snapshot predated the host's resolve. The old `body_checked_player` signal only captured attacker rebound, causing consistent under-correction; that signal is still emitted but is now exclusively used for server-side puck-strip dispatch via `SkaterController._on_body_checked_player`.

**`ClockSync`'s OFFSET does pure NTP only — do not add queue-depth feedback to the offset.** (The adaptive input-lead servo also lives in ClockSync as a companion, like `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` — but it only shapes future input *stamps*, never `_offset`; the ban below is about the clock.) The clock offset (`_offset`) is computed from ping/pong samples alone: collect up to 8 samples, drop the 2 highest-RTT outliers, average the rest, EMA-smooth (α=0.3) after `is_ready`. `estimated_host_time()` adds a monotone floor so time never goes backward. Do not nudge `_offset` for any other reason (buffer depth, queue length, etc.) — any unbounded integrator on the clock drifts without limit and makes the offset display useless. On same-machine sessions the offset will be approximately equal to the time between host start and client connection (e.g. +1.3 s if the client connected 1.3 s after the host started) — this is correct after the session-relative timestamp change. The important invariant is that the offset is **stable** (not drifting over time); ongoing drift is a code bug. Buffer depth is managed separately: the client receive buffer via `_adapt_interpolation_delay()` in each controller; the host input queue depth is governed by `INPUT_LEAD_SEC` in `ClockSync` (the constants live there because they are a companion to the offset; the method `estimated_input_stamp_time()` is the only call site).

**`facing` in `SkaterNetworkState` is `Vector2` (XZ packed as XY), not `Vector3`.** Both `WorldStateCodec` and the facing interpolation in `RemoteController` / `StateBufferManager` (`BufferedStateInterpolator.hermite_angle` on the extracted angle) use the same compass convention: extract angle via `atan2(x, y)` (bearing from +Z/forward axis) and reconstruct with `Vector2(sin, cos)`. Do not pass a `Vector3` facing direction to `lerp_facing`. `RemoteController` and `StateBufferManager` use `BufferedStateInterpolator.hermite_angle` for C1-continuous facing and upper-body rotation interpolation, driven by `facing_angular_velocity` and `upper_body_angular_velocity` fields on `SkaterNetworkState`.

**Wire timestamps are u32 in 0.1 ms units (`Constants.TIME_WIRE_SCALE`), not f32 seconds.** Applies to the world-state header, the skater block's `last_processed_ts`, input `host_timestamp`, and replay-file frame stamps. f32's ULP grows with host uptime (~1 ms at 2.3 h, ~2 ms at 4.6 h — at which point adjacent per-tick input stamps quantize equal and the dedupe drops real inputs); u32 @ 0.1 ms is constant-precision over ~119 h. Encode with `roundi` so grid round-trips are exact; `PredictedState.TS_MATCH_EPSILON` (1 ms) absorbs the ≤0.05 ms grid-vs-f64 error in reconcile lookups and history trims. Changing this representation means bumping `BuildInfo.PROTOCOL_VERSION` and `ReplayFileWriter.FORMAT_VERSION`.

**Claim stamps are validated against the host's own ping measurement at the RPC boundary.** `LagCompRewind.is_claim_stamp_plausible` (applied in `receive_pickup_claim` / `receive_poke_claim` / `receive_hit_claim`) rejects future stamps and stamps older than the peer's measured one-way + 100 ms slack. The resolvers' shared age fence, `LagCompRewind.claim_is_fresh` (200 ms), remains the absolute bound, but inside that window the earlier stamp wins contested pickups outright — without the per-peer check, backdating ~190 ms wins essentially every 50/50 puck.

**Host capture + broadcast run at END of tick (physics priority 2), after all actor integration.** `PostPhysicsNetHook` (a GameManager child at `process_physics_priority = 2` — after `SkaterController` at −1, autoloads/bodies at 0, `PuckController`/`Puck` at +1) invokes `GameManager._capture_and_broadcast_post_physics`, so each snapshot carries this tick's fully-integrated, phase-coherent state (position AND velocity post-move) labeled with the wall time it was produced, and ships the same tick. The pre-hook placement (GameManager's own `_physics_process`, which as an autoload runs before the scene's actors) captured the *previous* tick's result at the start of the next tick: one tick (~8.3 ms) of departure latency on every snapshot, velocity captured a phase ahead of position, and labels overstating content age by a tick — which the client's input-lead servo measured as pop-overdue and silently padded extra lead over. Don't move capture back to a pre-actor phase; if a new per-tick network step needs post-integration state, hang it off the hook (or a higher priority), not `_physics_process`. The broadcast CADENCE counter advances in `NetworkManager._physics_process` (priority 0) whether or not the hook runs, so a host stall's physics catch-up emits one broadcast per eligible tick with distinct `host_timestamp`s rather than one snap. Don't downshift the cadence per phase either — the client's jitter window assumes `STATE_RATE` packet spacing.

**All interpolators use host-capture timestamps, not client arrival time.** `apply_network_state` / `apply_state` on `RemoteController`, `PuckController`, and `GoalieController` each take a `host_ts: float` parameter decoded from the world-state header. All three buffer it as the snapshot timestamp. Using client arrival time instead of host-capture time causes same-frame world-state packets to silently clobber each other and decouples the render timeline from the simulation timeline. `RemoteController` and `GoalieController` render at `estimated_host_time() - interpolation_delay` off a single shared delay (`NetworkManager.get_interpolation_delay()`, advanced once per packet — not per-actor), so those two never drift relative to each other; the goalie dead-reckons its root by broadcast velocity only past the newest sample (`extrapolation_max_ms`), and runs no local AI. **The loose puck no longer shares that instant.** Since Phase 4b it renders *predicted to `estimated_host_time()`* (interpolation is its stale-data fallback only), so loose-puck-vs-agent relative timing is offset by a full `interp_delay` by design — see the puck-mode invariant above.

**Spectator slots use `team_id == GameRules.SPECTATOR_TEAM_ID` (-1) end-to-end.** Lobby keys spectators at `LobbySlotKey.SPECTATOR_KEY_BASE = 100` upward so they never collide with the 0..5 home/away keys; `LobbySlotKey.team_id`/`LobbySlotKey.slot` decode them. The `assign_player_slot` RPC carries `team_id = -1` for spectators (with zero-color tuples that the receiver ignores); `GameManager.on_slot_assigned` branches on this and calls `_become_local_spectator()` instead of `_registry.spawn`, mounting a `CameraDirector` (broadcast / chase / POV / free cams) and emitting `local_spectator_state_changed(true)` for HUD chrome. Spectators are deliberately NOT in `_registry`, so every iterator that already loops `_registry.all()` (scoreboard, off-screen indicators, TOI accumulator, career stats reporter, world-state codec) excludes them naturally — do not add explicit spectator filters to those callsites. The host-only mirror for cross-peer queries is `GameManager._spectator_peers: Dictionary[int, bool]`, populated in `on_host_started` / `on_player_connected` / `_push_lobby_assignments_to_clients` and exposed via `is_spectator_peer(peer_id)` and `spectator_peer_count()`. ENet connection cap is `GameRules.MAX_CONNECTIONS = MAX_PLAYERS + MAX_SPECTATORS`; the player roster is still gated separately by `PlayerRules.MAX_PER_TEAM`.

**Mid-game spectator ↔ player swap routes through `_on_slot_swap_requested` with separate paths.** `SlotSwapCoordinator.try_swap_slot` only validates peers already in `_state_machine.players`, so promote/demote bypass it: `new_team_id == -1` runs `_demote_player_to_spectator` (drops puck if carrier, broadcasts `notify_spectator_demoted`), `_spectator_peers.has(peer_id)` runs `_promote_spectator_to_player` (validates slot/team capacity inline, spawns + RPCs the same as `_push_lobby_assignments_to_clients`). The demote RPC is a one-way "despawn this peer's skater everywhere"; promote reuses the existing `spawn_remote_skater` + `assign_player_slot` RPCs that mid-game joins use, so other peers see promotion as if a fresh peer joined. The promoted peer's `on_slot_assigned` detects mid-game by `_state_machine != null` and skips `_spawn_world()`. Demote of the local peer **must clear the input batch provider before** `_registry.remove` queue-frees the LocalController — otherwise NetworkManager's input pump would call into a freed object.

**Reconnect reservations are host-authoritative, keyed by SteamID64, and count toward team totals.** When a non-bot peer with a Steam ID drops mid-match (not kicked — `NetworkManager.was_peer_kicked` gates it — and not at `GAME_OVER`), `GameManager._maybe_reserve_slot` snapshots their `{team_id, team_slot, stats, attributes}` into `_reserved_slots[steam_id]` and calls `_state_machine.reserve_slot`. The reserved `(team_id, team_slot)` counts in `count_players_on_team` (so the team plays short-handed rather than being backfilled) and is skipped by `_first_available_slot`. `request_join` carries the joiner's SteamID64 (added at `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 7; currently 8); on rejoin `on_player_connected` checks `_reserved_slots` **before** the spectator/roster-gate/auto-balance branches and routes a match to `_restore_reserved_player`, which re-locks the original attributes, respawns into the held slot, and carries the pre-drop stats forward. Expiry is a `RECONNECT_RESERVATION_S` (~60 s) `SceneTreeTimer` guarded by a monotonic token — `_expire_reservation` no-ops if the reservation was already claimed or re-reserved (token mismatch), so a re-drop's fresh timer can't tear down the new hold. Client side: a genuine mid-match connection loss (not a kick or graceful host shutdown) stashes `NetworkManager.pending_reconnect_lobby_id` (survives `reset()`), which `SideMenu` consumes on `_ready` to surface `LoadingScreen.show_reconnect` — Reconnect re-runs the join into the same lobby, Cancel falls back to free play.
