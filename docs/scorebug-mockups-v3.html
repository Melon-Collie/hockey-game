Scorebug v3 — Logo-anchored

Design language we're pulling from: Chunky outlined wordmark, three-color discipline (dark + ice-blue + white), sticker/decal energy, slight forward motion, sports-brand decal vibe (CCM/K2-era), vector-clean — no gradients. Each variant below tries to embody one specific aspect of this language. We can shift the "dark" from black toward a dark navy or even teal if the panel needs softening.

Forgetting the previous chyron-broadcast direction. Anchoring instead on the logo's design language: bold confident type, heavy edges, layered/sticker quality, slight motion, high contrast. All three variants here use the logo palette (dark navy + ice-blue + white) but cash in different aspects of the language. Team-color stripes still carry team identification — that's not negotiable for fast gameplay reading. The ice background matches the new in-game cool blue-gray tint.

Variant G Sticker Decal — outlined panel
What it borrows from the logo: the heavy outline. The whole panel is wrapped in a 1.5px ice-blue stroke (with a thin dark inset to give it depth), so it reads like a vinyl sticker stuck onto the screen. Drop shadow stays for offset separation from the ice. Most directly cashes in the logo's "decal" energy.

Variant H Speed Lean — slight forward italic skew
What it borrows from the logo: the motion/lean. The whole panel skews -3deg, with content counter-skewed back to keep text legible. Net result: the panel's box itself leans forward in the direction of play, like the logo wordmark's tilt. Subtler than it sounds — it reads as "this UI has momentum" not "this UI is broken." Most distinct silhouette of the three.

Variant I Outlined Type — every word is a sticker
What it borrows from the logo: the signature wordmark trick — heavy dark stroke around white type. AWAY / HOME / scores / clock all get a 1px black outline plus a 1-2px offset stroke shadow. Every text element reads like a mini-MITTS wordmark. Most direct typographic tie to the logo. Riskier because outlined text in HUDs can fight legibility if overdone; here it's only on the high-emphasis labels and numbers.

Honest read: the logo's three strongest design moves are (1) the heavy outline, (2) the slight motion/lean, and (3) the outlined wordmark trick. Each variant cashes in exactly one of those.

Variant G is probably the cleanest tie — the panel literally becomes a sticker. Lives well at 1000 hours of play because nothing is moving or distorted; it just looks confidently designed. Lowest risk.
Variant H is the boldest identity move — the lean is a strong silhouette signature. Could feel like "the game has personality" or could feel like "the HUD is broken" depending on tuning. Higher risk, higher reward.
Variant I is the most direct typographic homage to the logo, but outlined text can fight readability at small sizes (player names in scoreboard, etc.). Use sparingly.

My instinct: G, optionally combined with the slight italic on just the hero numerals (score / clock) from H so we get the sticker energy + a subtle motion accent without making the whole HUD lean. That's the design-language sweet spot.

Pick & mix is welcome — e.g., "G's outlined panel + I's outlined score numbers" is a real option.
